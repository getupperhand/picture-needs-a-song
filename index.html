02 · The definition

Sync ismusic marriedto picture.

Any time a song is synchronized to moving images, somebody licensed it. Film. Series. Ads. Trailers. Games. Promos. A brand's social post.

if it moves and it has music, that is a job
03 · Why it matters

Nobodyremembers thescene withoutthe song.

  • 01For the picture: a song tells an audience how to feel in three seconds. Brands and shows pay for that.
  • 02For you: a fee up front, royalties when it airs, and strangers looking you up by name.
  • 03For a career: one placement is a credit. Credits get the next call returned.
04 · The one thing to understand

Every song is two rights.Sync needs both.

Right one

The master

The recording itself. Whoever paid for and owns that specific recording. Licensed with a master use license.

Right two

The composition

The song underneath: melody and lyrics. Owned by the writers and their publishers. Licensed with a sync license.

1

The fee. Paid up front, split between the two sides.

2

The royalties. Paid by your PRO every time it airs.

05 · What it was

It used to bea locked door.

  • 01Supervisors called the major labels, the big publishers, and a handful of agents they already knew.
  • 02Clearing one song meant chasing every owner of both rights. It could take weeks.
  • 03If you were unsigned, you were not in the room. Nobody was being cruel. There was just no door.
06 · Music Dealers · Chicago · 2008

So we opened it.

Idea one

The open brief

A brand posts what it needs: the feeling, the references, the budget. Any independent artist can answer it.

Idea two

Pre-cleared, one stop

Artists confirmed they controlled both rights. A brand could say yes and use the song right away.

Idea three

You keep your song

Non-exclusive. The artist kept every copyright and could license the same song anywhere else.

07 · What it is now · 2026

One-stop won.

  • 01Pre-cleared is the default. Libraries and marketplaces built on it are everywhere.
  • 02There are more screens than ever: series, games, podcasts, creators, every brand's feed. All of it needs cleared music, fast.
  • 03The famous song still costs a fortune. The background is nearly free. Independent artists live in the middle, and the middle is big.
08 · Where it is going

Machines takethe background.People takethe scene.

Generated music will fill the space nobody listens to. That makes a real song, by a real person with a story and clean paperwork, worth more when the moment matters. Brands increasingly want the artist, not just the track.

09 · How to get placed, part one

The song thatclears fastestwins.

these two pick the easy yes
  • Control 100% of the master and the composition, or know exactly who does.
  • Splits agreed in writing before the song comes out. Every co-writer, every producer.
  • Registered with a PRO: ASCAP, BMI or SESAC. That is how royalties find you.
  • An instrumental, a clean version and stems, bounced and ready.
  • Metadata in the file: your contact, writers, splits, mood, tempo.
  • No uncleared samples. One will kill the deal.
10 · How to get placed, part two

Three doors in. Pitch thescene, not the song.

Door one

Libraries and marketplaces

Volume and reach. Usually non-exclusive. Read the terms, then upload your cleanest work.

Door two

Sync agents and publishers

They pitch for you and take a share. Worth it when they have relationships you do not.

Door three

Supervisors and brands, direct

Do the research. One short email, one link, no attachments. Say where the song fits.
